5. How calls work

5.1 Principles

  • Calls use WebRTC, built into browsers and the Android System WebView.
  • Audio and video go directly between the devices whenever the networks allow it. Otherwise they go through the TURN relay on the H_CHAT server, still encrypted (DTLS-SRTP): the relay forwards packets it can't read.
  • The backend only does signalling (ringing, offers/answers, network candidates) over Socket.IO. It never receives audio or video.
  • Every offer and answer is signed with the caller's/callee's identity key and verified by the other side (see Encryption §3.8).

5.2 1:1 call

Unanswered after 45 seconds → missed call (notification). If one side loses its connection for 15 seconds the call ends on both sides; if a device loses its socket for 30 seconds the server ends its calls.

5.3 Finding a path: direct or relayed

Network situation Typical path
Both on Wi-Fi at home / office Direct
One on mobile data (carrier NAT) Often relayed
Strict company Wi-Fi blocking UDP Relayed over TCP or TLS 5349

The call screen shows "direct" or "through the relay".

TURN credentials are short-lived (1 hour) and per user: username = "<expiry>:<userId>", password = HMAC-SHA1(TURN_SECRET, username) (coturn use-auth-secret). The relay refuses to forward to private, loopback and multicast addresses (denied-peer-ip), so it can't be used to reach the server's internal network.

5.4 Group calls (up to 6 people)

Group calls use a mesh: every device connects to every other device. There's no media server.

Rules: maximum 6 people at once (the 7th sees "Full"); members can join while it runs; the call ends when the starter ends it for everyone, or when only one person is left; nobody joins within 45 s → missed.

5.5 Audio quality: noise, echo and routing

Stage Where What
Capture Device getUserMedia with noise suppression, echo cancellation, automatic gain control (WebRTC Audio Processing Module, on the device)
Encode Device Opus (adaptive bitrate, packet-loss concealment)
Transport Network SRTP over UDP (or TCP/TLS through the relay)
Playback Device Separate <audio> element per remote person, restarted if Android pauses it
Routing (Android) CallAudioPlugin Headset/earbuds → earpiece → loudspeaker (on demand); call volume on the volume keys; screen off at the ear
Background (Android) CallService Foreground "call in progress" service keeps the microphone (and camera) with the screen off

Users can check their devices: in a call, Sound shows "their voice" and "your mic" level meters, lets them pick the output (by name) and the microphone, and plays a test sound. Settings › Calls › Test microphone and speaker records 3 seconds and plays them back.

5.6 Settings and rules

  • Receive calls (per user): off → callers see "isn't taking calls right now… for their own reasons. That's their choice: you can still write to them."
  • Calls are available in every category, voice and video, 1:1 and in groups.

5.7 Diagnostics

Each device reports, 5 s / 15 s / 30 s into a call, numbers only to the server log ("call audio"): bytes and packets received, audio energy, player state, output route, direct/relayed. No audio is ever sent. This is how the Android earpiece and paused-player problems were found and fixed.
